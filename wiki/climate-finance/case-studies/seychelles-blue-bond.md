# Seychelles Blue Bond

> In 2018, the Seychelles issued the world's first sovereign blue bond — raising $15 million to protect its oceans and pioneering a new instrument for small island states facing existential climate risk.

## What Happened

On 29 October 2018, the Government of Seychelles launched the world's first sovereign blue bond, raising $15 million from international investors. The bond was supported by a $5 million partial credit guarantee and a $5 million concessional loan from the World Bank — both designed to lower the Seychelles' borrowing costs and make the deal financially viable for a small island economy.

The proceeds were channelled through two dedicated funds: the Blue Grants Fund (for conservation projects) and the Blue Investment Fund (for commercial sustainable fisheries). Both were managed by domestic institutions — the Seychelles Conservation and Climate Adaptation Trust (SeyCCAT) and the Development Bank of Seychelles — ensuring the capital stayed connected to local governance. Target activities included expanding marine protected areas, improving management of priority fisheries, and developing the Seychelles' broader blue economy.

The bond had a ten-year maturity and targeted institutional investors in the US market. It was structured with technical assistance from the World Bank and Nature Conservancy. The Seychelles had already undertaken a debt-for-nature swap in 2016 — converting $21.6 million of sovereign debt into conservation commitments — and the blue bond built on that foundation.

## Why It Matters for Comms

The Seychelles case is one of the most compelling origin stories in climate finance because it inverts the usual power dynamic. Here is one of the world's smallest economies — a nation of 100,000 people spread across 115 islands — pioneering a financial instrument that has since been replicated by countries including Belize, Palau, and Tonga. The country that has done almost nothing to cause climate change is doing sophisticated things to respond to it.

For communications professionals, the blue bond offers something rare in climate finance: a genuinely accessible human story. The Seychelles depends on healthy oceans for its food security, tourism, and livelihoods. The bond connects abstract financial mechanisms to tangible local realities — coral reefs, fisheries, coastal communities. It is the kind of case study that works in a keynote, a media pitch, or an investor brief because it is both technically interesting and emotionally legible.

The case also illustrates the role of [[Development Finance Institutions]] in making innovative instruments possible. Without the World Bank's guarantee and concessional financing, the deal's economics would not have worked. That blended structure — public money de-risking private capital — is the template for climate finance in vulnerable and developing economies.

## Key Facts

- 29 October 2018: World's first sovereign blue bond issued
- Amount: $15 million, 10-year maturity
- Issuer: Government of Seychelles
- Support: $5M World Bank credit guarantee + $5M concessional loan
- Proceeds: marine protected areas, fisheries management, blue economy development
- Managed through SeyCCAT (grants) and Development Bank of Seychelles (investment)
- Built on a 2016 debt-for-nature swap ($21.6M of sovereign debt converted to conservation commitments)
- Inspired sovereign blue bond issuances by Belize, Palau, and Tonga
