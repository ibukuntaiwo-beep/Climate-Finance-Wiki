# Lombard Odier: Ice Cubes and Burning Logs

> Swiss private bank Lombard Odier developed a four-part framework for thinking about companies and climate — and gave it a metaphor vivid enough to become a standard reference in investment communications.

## The Framework

Lombard Odier's investment team uses a model called Climate Value Impact (CVI) to classify companies into four categories based on their emissions profile and their trajectory:

**Burning logs** — companies operating in high-emitting sectors that have no credible decarbonisation plan. They are misaligned with net zero and carry growing transition risk as carbon prices rise and regulations tighten.

**Ice cubes** — companies that also operate in high-emitting sectors but are actively and rapidly decarbonising. They carry transition risk by sector association, but their trajectory is toward lower emissions, and Lombard Odier argues they will gain competitive advantage as the transition accelerates.

**Solutions providers** — companies whose own carbon footprint is outweighed by the climate mitigation solutions they offer. Think renewable energy equipment, energy storage, or precision agriculture technology.

**Climate insulated** — companies whose business model is largely unaffected by climate risk in either direction.

The insight at the heart of the framework is that the right question is not "is this company currently green?" but "which direction is it moving, and how fast?" An ice cube operating in steel or cement today may be a better climate investment than a solutions provider with flat growth, precisely because the transition value is being created in the act of decarbonising.

## Why It Matters for Comms

Lombard Odier's framework matters less for its financial methodology than for its language. "Ice cubes and burning logs" achieves something rare in climate finance communication: it makes a complex, counterintuitive idea instantly memorable. The metaphor carries the argument — a burning log is going to ash; an ice cube is melting but it starts from a cold place. Both are temporary states. The investment question is which direction each is heading.

For communications professionals, this is a case study in how to make abstract financial concepts accessible without dumbing them down. The four-part typology also solves a practical problem that clients often face: how do you explain why a fund owns companies in heavy industry or fossil fuels while claiming climate alignment? The ice cube concept provides a narrative — engagement and transition, not exclusion.

The framework also maps cleanly onto the debate between divestment and engagement strategies that runs through much of climate investing. Burning logs are candidates for exclusion; ice cubes are candidates for engagement and investment during their transition. Solutions providers are the obvious "green" holdings. Climate insulated companies are handled on conventional financial grounds. Each category has a different communications posture.

## Key Facts

- Framework: Climate Value Impact (CVI) — four categories: burning logs, ice cubes, solutions providers, climate insulated
- Developed by Lombard Odier Investment Managers (LOIM), the asset management arm of the Swiss private bank
- Central thesis: trajectory matters more than current emissions profile
- "Ice cubes": high-emitting companies in active, rapid decarbonisation
- "Burning logs": high-emitting companies with no credible transition plan
- LOIM runs the TargetNetZero equity strategy and the Global Climate Bond fund using this framework
- The podcast series "Ice cubes, burning logs and the road to net zero" (2022) expanded the framework publicly
