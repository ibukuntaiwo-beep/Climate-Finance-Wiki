# ENEL and the Birth of the SLB Market

> In September 2019, Italian energy giant ENEL issued the world's first major corporate sustainability-linked bond — a deal that created an entirely new market category and changed how climate finance works.

## What Happened

ENEL is one of Europe's largest electricity companies, operating across more than 30 countries. In September 2019, it issued a $1.5 billion bond in the US market with an unusual feature: the interest rate was tied to whether ENEL hit a specific sustainability target. If the company failed to reach 55% renewable capacity in its total installed base by the end of 2021, the coupon would step up by 25 basis points — meaning investors would receive a higher return as compensation for ENEL's underperformance.

This was the first time a major corporate bond had structured financial consequences directly around a sustainability metric. The KPIs referenced two UN Sustainable Development Goals: SDG 7 (Affordable and Clean Energy) and SDG 13 (Climate Action). The deal was priced 10 basis points inside ENEL's conventional bond curve — early evidence of what would become known as [[The Greenium]].

The response from investors was immediate. The deal was heavily oversubscribed and drew a new category of ESG-focused buyers. ENEL went on to build one of the largest SLB programmes of any corporate issuer globally, eventually tying multiple bonds to targets including renewable capacity and carbon intensity. By late 2023, however, ENEL was forced to raise coupons on approximately $11 billion of bonds after missing several of its sustainability performance targets — making it also the defining case study in what happens when SLB commitments are tested.

## Why It Matters for Comms

The ENEL story has two chapters, and both matter for communications professionals. The first chapter — creating the [[Sustainability-Linked Bonds|SLB]] market — illustrates how a single deal can define a new instrument category. ENEL's innovation was conceptual as much as financial: separating the *use of proceeds* question (where does the money go?) from the *outcomes* question (what does the issuer actually achieve?). That shift unlocked climate finance for companies in hard-to-abate sectors that couldn't easily ring-fence capital for green projects.

The second chapter — the coupon step-ups — is equally instructive. ENEL missing its targets was not a scandal; it was the mechanism working as designed. Investors received higher returns; ENEL paid a financial price for underperformance. But it raised uncomfortable questions: were the original targets ambitious enough? Was a 25bp step-up a meaningful deterrent? The market's credibility depends on the answer. Critics who had flagged that some SLB targets were weak found their concerns validated.

For comms professionals advising on SLB issuance or corporate climate commitments, the ENEL case sets the benchmark for what rigorous target-setting looks like — and what accountability looks like when targets are missed.

## Key Facts

- September 2019: ENEL issues $1.5 billion SLB — world's first major corporate SLB
- KPI: 55% renewable capacity share by end of 2021
- Coupon step-up: +25 basis points if KPI missed
- Priced 10bp inside conventional curve — early evidence of a greenium
- Deal was heavily oversubscribed; drew new ESG investor base
- SLB market grew to $500B+ in issuance within four years
- 2023–2024: ENEL triggers coupon step-ups on ~$11 billion of bonds after missing targets
- ICMA published Sustainability-Linked Bond Principles in 2020, partly in response to market growth sparked by ENEL
