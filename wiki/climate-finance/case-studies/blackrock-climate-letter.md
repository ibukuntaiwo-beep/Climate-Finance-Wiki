# BlackRock's Climate Ultimatum

> When the world's largest asset manager declared climate risk a core investment concern in January 2020, it triggered a reorientation of mainstream finance. What followed was more complicated.

## What Happened

On 14 January 2020, Larry Fink published his annual letter to CEOs. The title was "A Fundamental Reshaping of Finance." The core argument was precise: climate risk is investment risk, and because climate change is going to reshape the economy over coming decades, it will reshape the value of every asset class. BlackRock, Fink declared, would put sustainability at the centre of its investment approach.

The letter set out concrete commitments. BlackRock would exit investments in companies that generated more than 25% of revenues from thermal coal production. It would launch new investment products that screened out fossil fuels. It would demand that companies report in line with the recommendations of the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) and the Sustainability Accounting Standards Board (SASB). Climate risk would become a standard factor in portfolio construction and risk management across the firm.

The reaction was immediate. Because BlackRock managed approximately $7 trillion in assets, its declaration that climate is a financial risk was not a niche investor position. It was, effectively, a signal to every CEO and CFO in every major market that the world's largest asset manager was now evaluating their companies through a climate lens.

The following year, BlackRock supported Engine No. 1's activist campaign against ExxonMobil, a direct application of its stated climate governance principles. The period from 2020 to 2021 represented the high-water mark of mainstream financial institutions treating climate as a defining investment question.

The tide then shifted. In 2022, BlackRock announced it would support fewer climate-related shareholder resolutions, stating some were not consistent with clients' long-term financial interests. A Morningstar analysis found BlackRock voted against 80% of key climate resolutions. Republican-led US states began withdrawing public pension funds over what they characterised as politically motivated ESG investing, withdrawing an estimated $4 billion in 2022 alone. In June 2023, Fink told an interviewer he was "ashamed" of the term ESG and would stop using it.

## Why It Matters for Comms

The BlackRock letter is the single most important document in the normalisation of climate as a financial variable. Before January 2020, climate risk was a concern for specialist ESG funds and NGO campaigns. After it, the world's largest asset manager had put it in writing to every major CEO on earth.

For communications professionals, the case illustrates both the power and the fragility of institutional climate commitments. BlackRock's retreat from ESG language in 2023 did not mean the underlying financial logic had changed: climate risk is still investment risk. What changed was the political cost of saying so in certain markets. Knowing the difference between a strategic reorientation and a positioning move is essential for anyone communicating on behalf of financial institutions making climate commitments.

The case also introduced a structural tension that now runs through all large passive fund climate claims: a passive index fund cannot divest from the companies in its benchmark. BlackRock's actual leverage was through voting and engagement, not capital withdrawal. Any communications work involving passive fund managers and climate commitments needs to understand this distinction clearly.

## Key Facts

- 14 January 2020: Larry Fink publishes "A Fundamental Reshaping of Finance" to CEOs worldwide
- BlackRock managed approximately $7 trillion in assets at time of letter
- Commitments included: exit from companies with >25% thermal coal revenues, new fossil-fuel-screened products, demand for TCFD reporting
- January 2020: client letter confirms exit from thermal coal producers and new screened products
- May 2021: BlackRock votes in favour of Engine No. 1's board nominees at ExxonMobil
- 2022: BlackRock signals retreat, supports fewer climate resolutions; Republican states withdraw ~$4 billion in protest
- June 2023: Fink publicly distances himself from the term ESG
