# Ubiquitous Language — Climate Finance

Canonical glossary for this wiki. This is the single source of truth for how terms are used across all articles, queries, and outputs.

When a user or owner uses a synonym or informal label, map it to the canonical term here on first use (e.g., "Paris deal (Paris Agreement)"), then continue with the canonical term.

---

## Core Concepts

| Canonical Term | Common Synonyms / Variants | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Climate Finance** | green finance, sustainable finance, ESG finance | The flow of capital — public, private, and blended — directed at reducing emissions, building resilience, and managing climate-related financial risk. |
| **Mitigation** | decarbonisation, emissions reduction, cutting emissions | Actions that reduce or prevent greenhouse gas emissions. Distinct from adaptation. |
| **Adaptation** | climate resilience, climate-proofing | Actions that reduce vulnerability to the impacts of climate change already locked in. |
| **Physical Risks** | climate physical risk, acute/chronic risk | Financial risks arising from the direct physical effects of climate change (storms, floods, sea-level rise, heat). Includes acute (event-driven) and chronic (gradual) risks. |
| **Transition Risks** | policy risk, stranded asset risk, low-carbon transition risk | Financial risks arising from the shift to a low-carbon economy: policy changes, technology disruption, market repricing, and reputational shifts. |
| **Stranded Assets** | stranded asset risk, write-down risk | Assets that have lost value or must be retired early due to climate policy, regulation, or market shifts — most commonly fossil fuel reserves and infrastructure. |
| **Net Zero** | net-zero emissions, carbon neutrality (see note) | A state in which greenhouse gas emissions entering the atmosphere are balanced by removals. Typically refers to a specific target year (e.g., Net Zero by 2050). |
| **Carbon Neutral** | carbon neutrality, climate neutral | Achieving zero net carbon dioxide emissions, typically by offsetting residual emissions. Distinct from Net Zero, which covers all greenhouse gases. |
| **The 1.5°C Threshold** | 1.5 degrees, Paris temperature goal | The aspirational warming limit set by the Paris Agreement. Breached on an annual basis in 2024. |
| **Greenwashing** | green claims, ESG washing | Making misleading or unsubstantiated claims about the environmental credentials of a product, service, or institution. |

## Instruments

| Canonical Term | Common Synonyms / Variants | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Green Bonds** | climate bonds, green debt | Bonds whose proceeds are ring-fenced exclusively for qualifying environmental projects, governed by the Green Bond Principles. |
| **Sustainability-Linked Bonds** | SLBs, KPI-linked bonds | Bonds whose financial terms (typically the coupon) change if the issuer hits or misses pre-agreed sustainability targets. Proceeds are not ring-fenced. |
| **Transition Bonds** | climate transition bonds | Bonds designed to finance the decarbonisation of high-emitting sectors that cannot yet qualify for green bonds. |
| **Carbon Offsets** | carbon credits, carbon removals, offsets | Credits representing one tonne of CO2 (or equivalent) reduced or removed from the atmosphere, used to compensate for emissions elsewhere. |
| **The Greenium** | green bond premium | The lower borrowing cost (spread differential) that green bond issuers may achieve compared to conventional bonds of equivalent credit quality. |
| **Blended Finance** | public-private blending, catalytic capital | The strategic use of public or philanthropic capital to de-risk and mobilise private investment in climate or development outcomes. |

## Standards & Frameworks

| Canonical Term | Common Synonyms / Variants | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Paris Agreement** | Paris deal, Paris accord, Paris climate deal | The 2015 international treaty under the UNFCCC committing signatories to limit global warming to well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C, and enhance resilience. |
| **TCFD** | Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures | A framework (now integrated into IFRS S2) for companies to disclose climate-related risks and opportunities across governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics. |
| **IFRS S1 and S2** | ISSB standards, sustainability disclosure standards | The International Sustainability Standards Board's standards for general sustainability (S1) and climate-specific (S2) disclosure. S2 incorporates TCFD. |
| **CSRD** | Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive | EU regulation requiring large companies to report on sustainability topics under European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS). |
| **EU Taxonomy** | EU green taxonomy, taxonomy regulation | The EU's classification system defining what economic activities qualify as environmentally sustainable. Underpins the EU Green Bond Standard. |
| **Green Bond Principles** | GBP, ICMA GBP | Voluntary guidelines administered by ICMA setting out best practice for green bond issuance across four pillars: use of proceeds, project selection, proceeds management, reporting. |
| **Science Based Targets** | SBTi, science-based targets | Emissions reduction targets validated by the Science Based Targets initiative as consistent with limiting warming to 1.5°C or well below 2°C. |

## Actors

| Canonical Term | Common Synonyms / Variants | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **IPCC** | Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change | The UN body that synthesises global climate science and produces Assessment Reports used as the scientific baseline for policy and finance. |
| **UNFCCC and COP** | the UNFCCC, COP process, climate negotiations | The UN Framework Convention on Climate Change and its annual Conference of the Parties — the primary international forum for climate agreements. |
| **GFANZ** | Glasgow Financial Alliance for Net Zero | A coalition of leading financial institutions committed to transitioning their lending and investment portfolios to net zero by 2050. |
| **MDBs** | multilateral development banks, development finance institutions (DFIs) | Institutions like the World Bank and regional development banks that use public capital to mobilise private climate finance, especially in emerging markets. |
| **Climate Action 100+** | CA100+, investor climate coalition | An investor-led initiative pressing the world's largest corporate emitters to take action on climate change. |

## Usage notes

- **Net Zero vs Carbon Neutral:** These are not interchangeable. Net Zero covers all greenhouse gases; Carbon Neutral typically refers to CO2 only and may rely heavily on offsets. Use the precise term and flag the distinction where relevant.
- **Climate Finance vs Sustainable Finance vs ESG:** These overlap but are not identical. Climate Finance is the narrowest (climate-specific). Sustainable Finance is broader (includes social). ESG is a framework for integrating environmental, social, and governance factors — not a finance category in itself.
- **Offsets vs Direct Finance:** Carbon offsets compensate for emissions elsewhere; they do not directly finance climate solutions. Do not conflate them with green bonds or blended finance.
