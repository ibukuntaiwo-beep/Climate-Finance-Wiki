Overview

Adopted 12 December 2015 at COP21 in Paris, entered into force November 2016. Replaced the Kyoto Protocol — fixed its biggest flaw by requiring participation from all countries, not just wealthy ones. 195 parties ratified.

Three headline goals: limit warming to well below 2°C while pursuing 1.5°C; strengthen adaptation; align financial flows with low-carbon development. That last goal makes it a climate finance document as much as a climate one.

The Agreement calls for a balance between emissions and removals in the second half of the century — the legal formulation of net zero.

How It Works: NDCs and the Ratchet

The core mechanism is the Nationally Determined Contribution. Each country sets its own target and submits to the UNFCCC. No penalty for missing an NDC, but a legally binding obligation to have one and update it.

The ratchet mechanism requires progressively more ambitious NDCs every five years. A Global Stocktake every five years assesses collective progress — the first, at COP28 (2023), concluded the world was off track.

Finance and the $100 Billion Goal

Article 2.1(c) calls for “making finance flows consistent with a pathway towards low greenhouse gas emissions.” Developed countries pledged $100B/year for developing nations by 2020 — technically met, though disputed.

A new goal (NCQG) is being negotiated in the trillions. The Just Transition dimension — who pays, who benefits — remains deeply contested.

Comms Insight
When a company or fund says it is “Paris-aligned,” it is claiming consistency with the 1.5°C or well-below-2°C goal. That is a specific, testable claim — not a vague aspiration. Ask what methodology they are using to verify it.

Carbon Markets Under the Agreement

Article 6 creates the framework for international carbon markets. Rules for Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes (ITMOs) were only finalised at COP29 in 2024.

You Might Not Expect
The Paris Agreement is as much about money as about temperature
Article 2.1(c) — the finance flows clause — is the legal basis for everything from GFANZ to the EU Taxonomy to central bank climate stress tests. The temperature targets get the headlines, but the finance clause is doing more work.
